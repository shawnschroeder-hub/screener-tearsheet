Want a true yellow and a new truck?

  • Order a 2026 Bronco in Oxford White or Wimbledon White and have it wrapped yellow. A quality full wrap runs about $3,500 to $5,500 in the Chicago area, lasts 5 to 7 years, protects the paint and peels off at resale.
  • Orange Fury (2026) is a factory alternative in the same warm family. Shows in the configurator on Big Bend, Outer Banks and Badlands.
  • Skip the aftermarket respray: $8,000+ and it hurts resale.

Before she buys any Bronco

  • Pull the Ford OASIS warranty history by VIN at the dealer: confirms whether a 2021 to early-2022 2.7 V6 had its intake-valve engine replacement, and whether the hardtop was swapped under the 2021 recall.
  • Prefer a 2.3-liter four for commuting miles: cheaper, 1 to 2 mpg better, and it sidestepped the 2.7 valve problem.
  • Sasquatch 35-inch tires cost about 2 mpg, add road noise and run $1,600 a set. Great for the look, bad for 25,000 miles a year.

How to decide

The honest read on a Bronco for 25,000 miles a year

  • Gas: 19 to 21 mpg means about $5,838 a year at today's Illinois prices. A RAV4 Hybrid is $2,994. Over five years that gap is a used car.
  • Noise: the Bronco's flat windshield and removable roof make it the loudest vehicle on this board at highway speed. Outer Banks with the sound-deadening headliner helps. Test-drive it at 70 mph before falling in love.
  • Reliability: Consumer Reports rates the Bronco average, ahead of the Wrangler and well behind the 4Runner and RAV4. Known issues: 2021 to early-2022 2.7 V6 intake valves (engine replacements under warranty, fixed in production October 2021), early hardtops replaced under recall, and tie rods on hard off-road use.
  • Resale: strong. Broncos hold value better than almost anything but a 4Runner, which softens the gas bill when she sells.
  • If it has to be a Bronco: 2023 or newer, 2.3 engine, 4-door, Big Bend or Outer Banks, no Sasquatch, hardtop, and a Ford Power Protect extended warranty if she is buying used out of warranty.

My ranking for her

  1. Toyota RAV4 Hybrid (or CR-V Hybrid if she wants more room). Cheapest to own by a mile, AWD, bulletproof. Buy a 2022 to 2024 one under $28,000 or a new 2026 at sticker.
  2. Toyota Sienna if she really does live out of the car: nothing else combines 36 mpg with a flat 8-foot floor.
  3. Toyota 4Runner (2025+) for the rugged look without the Bronco's noise and repair record. Pay the premium once, keep it 15 years.
  4. Yellow Bronco: the Lafayette Yellowstone Heritage Edition at $53,500 or the Belleville Cyber Orange Metallic Tricoat Outer Banks at $32,513, with eyes open about gas and noise. It is the only one on this list she will smile at every morning, and that counts for something.

How to shop these

  • Every "View" button opens the live Autotrader listing. Prices move daily; the Lafayette Yellowstone Heritage Edition at $53,500 or the Belleville Cyber Orange Metallic Tricoat Outer Banks at $32,513 are the ones to call first.
  • For any used car: ask for the OASIS report (Ford), Toyota or Honda service history by VIN, and get a $150 to $200 independent pre-purchase inspection. Walk away from anything with a branded title.
  • Out-of-state purchases (Indiana, Wisconsin, Michigan, Ohio) are fine: the dealer collects no sales tax and she pays Illinois tax at titling. Budget a day for the drive.
  • Financing: get a credit-union pre-approval first (Consumers Credit Union, Alliant), then let the dealer try to beat it. Ford Credit runs 1.9% to 3.9% offers on 2026 Broncos most months.
