Uber Car Payback

Which cars can you buy, drive for Uber around Chicago, pay off, and still have money left every month. Every row is a used car you can buy near Bourbonnais today; the numbers recompute when you pick how many hours a week you would drive. Built 2026-10-13, updated 2026-10-16 with the Tesla plan and the where-to-drive map.

The short answer

Yes, if the car is a used hybrid or EV and you drive about 15 hours a week or more. A used Toyota Prius, Corolla Hybrid or Camry Hybrid pays for itself (loan, insurance, gas and wear) in roughly 6 to 9 hours of driving a week. Every hour after that is profit of about $18 to $22.

Best money-makers: Camry Hybrid, Accord Hybrid or Sonata Hybrid once you qualify for Uber Comfort (100 trips and a 4.85 rating), because Comfort pays about 20% more per trip. A used 2023 Tesla Model 3 posts the highest number of all if you can charge at home. Cheapest way in: a used Chevy Bolt EV at about $15,000 after the Illinois rebate. Avoid: a cheap gas car (the gas bill erases the lower payment) and renting through Hertz for more than a trial month or two (about $1,300 a month, 50% more hours to break even, and you own nothing at the end).

Your plan: a Tesla that Uber pays for

The car: a 2023 Model 3 RWD from Tesla's own used inventory, clean history only (no "Repaired" badge), around $24,000, less the Illinois $2,000 used-EV rebate when an Illinois dealer sells it. Payment about $425 a month on a 60-month loan; insurance about $250 a month with a rideshare add-on. The Tesla board from 10/11 has the shopping details.

Home charging: a Level 2 charger in the garage is a must. ComEd's 2026 residential rebate covers up to $2,500 of charger plus installation, which normally covers the whole job. Charging at home costs about 4 to 5 cents a mile. There is no Supercharger within 45 miles of Bourbonnais, so you will not be fast-charging on the road.

Comfort Electric: Chicago is a Comfort Electric market and the Model 3 is one of the named cars for it. Once you have 100 trips and a 4.85 rating, you get Comfort and Comfort Electric requests on top of UberX, which pay roughly 20% more per trip.

The airport worry, with numbers

A one-way airport trip is not a waste, even if you drive back empty. Example: Orland Park to Midway is about 20 miles. Uber charges riders roughly $52–60 on routes that length out of Orland Park, and you keep roughly $35–40 of it plus the tip. Driving back to Orland Park empty costs you 20 miles at 20 cents, about $4. You still cleared about $30 for 50 minutes of work.

  • Rematch comes first. Chicago made rematch permanent at both airports in April 2025. When you drop off at Midway the app offers you a waiting rider within 5–10 seconds; accept it and loop around Kilpatrick Ave to the lower level. No lot, no wait.
  • If there is no rematch, you can join the staging lot at 7101 S. Cicero (first in, first out; the app shows your place in line) or skip it. Friday and Sunday afternoons the queue moves fast; midweek midday it can be an hour. Leaving driver mode loses your spot.
  • Point the app home. Uber lets you set a destination twice a day. Set it to Orland Park or Bourbonnais after the drop-off and you only get requests heading your way. Trips to the south suburbs from Midway are common on Friday evenings and Sunday nights when people fly home.
  • Worst case: Midway back to Bourbonnais empty is 55 miles, about $11 in the Tesla. Even that leaves you ahead on the trip. In a gas car it would be about $25, which is why the EV makes airport runs easier to say yes to.
  • Don't chase O'Hare. It is 70 miles from home. Take it only if a rider brings you there, then rematch or set a destination filter south.

    How the math works

    What's left each month = Uber pay for the hours you pick, minus the loan payment, minus insurance, minus the per-mile cost of running the car (fuel, upkeep and tires, and the value the extra miles take off the car). We assume 17 miles driven for every hour online, including the empty miles between riders.

    • Loan: used-car rate of 7.5% for 60 months with 10% down; 7% added for sales tax and fees. The payment buys the car; the "wear" charge covers what Uber miles knock off its resale value.
    • Insurance: $180–250 a month for full coverage plus a rideshare add-on on a dedicated car (Illinois averages $235 for full coverage; State Farm and Auto-Owners are the cheap quotes). If this replaces a car you already insure, your real cost is lower and every row looks better.
    • Fuel: gas at $4.67 (AAA Illinois, Oct 13). Electricity at about 5 cents a mile (ComEd home rate with some public charging).
    • Hours to break even = how many hours a week you must drive before the car has paid its own loan, insurance and running costs. Everything past that is yours.
    • Taxes: the IRS lets you deduct 76 cents for every business mile (second half of 2026). At 17 miles an hour that deduction is larger than most of your profit, so you will owe little income or self-employment tax on this money. Keep a mileage log from day one.

    Uber's rules in Chicago: 2011 or newer, 4 doors, 5 factory seats, no salvage title, no cosmetic damage, working A/C. Comfort needs a 2019-or-newer car with at least 36 inches of rear legroom. XL needs 7 seats. Driving inside Chicago city limits needs a TNP chauffeur license, which Uber arranges, plus the windshield decal.

    Where you live matters: the $22 an hour is a Chicago-metro number. Kankakee and Bourbonnais have far fewer ride requests, so plan on driving the south suburbs, Midway or O'Hare runs, or weekend nights downtown for these numbers to hold. Try it for a month on a Hertz rental before buying if you are unsure.
