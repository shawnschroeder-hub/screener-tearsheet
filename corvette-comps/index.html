Facebook Marketplace searched city by city (Chicago, Dallas, LA, New York, Atlanta, Miami, Detroit, Philadelphia, Houston, Seattle, Nashville, Boston, Minneapolis, St. Louis, Kansas City, Las Vegas, San Francisco, Tampa), every 1969 Corvette on ClassicCars.com (61 listings), Hemmings (52), Classics on Autotrader (57), plus Streetside Classics, Gateway, Schmitt, TheVetteNet, Hagerty auction results.
  • What counts as a comp. A factory 427 car (L36, L68 or L71) that still has a 427 in it and is sold as mostly original. Cars with 454/496/502 swaps, LS swaps, pro-touring builds, L88/L89/ZL1 cars and $150k+ restomods are shown separately or left out, because they do not price like Shawn's car.
  • Asking is not selling. Dealer asks on this page run roughly 10% to 20% over what cars actually trade for; the five sales (two Hagerty auctions, three dealer sold-tags) are the anchors. The Hagerty L71 convertible that hammered at $44,405 in Feb 2025 is the clearest warning that 435-hp badges do not guarantee $80k.
  • Fathom Green. Code 983, offered only in 1969. Three of the five sold anchors are Fathom Green 427 cars at $49,990 to $59,900. Two more Fathom Green big blocks on the market today were repainted (Vineland NJ, now LeMans Blue; Milford CT, now black at $89,000), which is why an unmolested factory-color car has a story to sell.
  • Mileage. 58,000 miles is right in the pack: the closest comps run 17k to 106k and price barely moves with miles once a car is restored. Documentation (tank sticker, Protect-O-Plate, NCRS judging) moves it more.
