Go straight to eero 7 if:
  • The TV is a long way from the box, such as two floors or the opposite end of a large house.
  • You tried the Xfinity extender and the TV still buffers. Ask Xfinity about returns before you order one.
  • You may switch internet providers and want to keep what you bought.

Try these free steps first

  1. Measure at the TV. Stand where the TV is, join your Wi-Fi on a phone and open fast.com. Note the number, or that it will not connect.
  2. Move the Xfinity box. Get it out of any cabinet or basement corner, up off the floor and closer to the middle of the house. Test again.
  3. Ask Xfinity. Ask whether your account already includes a WiFi Extender, and which gateway model you have.
  4. Look behind the TV. A round cable outlet or a network jack nearby opens up a wired fix.

Setting up the Xfinity Extender

  • Plug it in about halfway between the Xfinity box and the TV, where the signal is still good.
  • In the Xfinity app, tap More, then Add a Device, then WiFi Extender.
  • Advanced Security has to be turned on in the app, and the box must not be in Bridge Mode.
  • If the TV has a network port and the extender sits close enough, connect them with a short cable.

Setting up a mesh like eero

  • Plug the first unit into the Xfinity box with a cable. Put the second where it still gets a good signal, about halfway to the TV or a bit past that.
  • For one clean network, turn on Bridge Mode on the Xfinity box (10.0.0.1). That turns off the box's own Wi-Fi, and Xfinity's extenders and Wi-Fi controls stop working.
  • If Bridge Mode is missing or you want to keep Xfinity's features, set the mesh to access point mode in its app instead. Forum reports on whether the newest XB10 box allows Bridge Mode conflict.
